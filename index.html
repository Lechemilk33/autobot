A 2025 federal law lets many tipped and hourly workers deduct up to $25,000 of tips and up to $12,500 of overtime premium from their taxable income. Most people misjudge what that means for their refund — in both directions. This calculator does the actual math.

What this estimate does and doesn't include

It computes your two deductions under the 2025 law — the caps, the high-income phase-down, and the exact federal tax brackets for the year you picked, stacked on top of the standard deduction. It assumes the income you entered is all taxed as ordinary income and that you take the standard deduction.

It does not know about your credits, itemized deductions, retirement contributions, state taxes, or anything else on your return, and it can't verify that your tips or overtime qualify. Social Security and Medicare taxes still come out of tips and overtime either way — this deduction only reduces federal income tax. Treat the result as a good estimate, not a promise, and not tax advice.

Why your paycheck didn't change

Employers still withhold income tax from tips and overtime as usual. The deduction shows up when you file your return — typically as a bigger refund. If you'd rather get the money during the year, you can file an updated Form W-4 with your employer and lower your withholding.

Claiming it for 2026: your W-2 is the gatekeeper

For tax year 2026, the overtime deduction is limited to the amount your employer reports in W-2 Box 12, code TT, and tips flow from code TP. If those boxes are missing or wrong, your deduction shrinks with them — so check your W-2 against your own records in January, and talk to payroll early if you're earning qualified overtime now.
