For employers & bookkeepers · W-2 Box 12 code TT

Get the code TT number right, for every employee at once.

Starting with 2026 W-2s, you must report each employee's qualified overtime compensation — and it is not their overtime pay. It's only the federally required premium: half the regular rate, for hours past 40 a week. Report the full time-and-a-half by mistake and your employees' deductions (and your W-2c corrections) become your problem in February.

The rule, in one example

An employee earns a regular rate of $20/hour and works 10 overtime hours. You pay time-and-a-half: $300 of overtime wages. The amount for code TT is $100 — the ten "half" premiums of $10 — not $300. If you generously pay double time ($400), code TT is still $100: only the premium federal law requires ever qualifies. This matches the IRS's own worked example in its August 2026 employer FAQ.

What this tool can't check: whether each employee is actually covered by federal overtime law (salaried-exempt staff aren't), and whether your "regular rate" already includes required extras like nondiscretionary bonuses and shift differentials — under federal rules those raise the regular rate, and therefore the premium. If you pay bonuses to overtime workers, compute the adjusted regular rate first, then use it here. When in doubt, confirm with your payroll provider or accountant.
Why not just let my payroll software do it?

The big payroll platforms are adding this — but bookkeepers keep finding systems that report full overtime wages, include non-FLSA overtime (daily or contractual), or haven't turned the field on. This gives you an independent number to check theirs against before W-2s go out. Two minutes now against a stack of W-2c corrections later.
