The one thing everyone gets wrong

No form does not mean no tax. Income from selling and gig work is taxable whether or not any paperwork shows up. The forms only change what the tax agencies already know about — not what you owe. Selling your own used stuff at a loss (most casual closet-clearing) generally creates no tax, even when a 1099-K arrives; you report the form and offset it.

Money from friends and family

Personal transfers — splitting rent, paying back dinner, gifts — are not income and should never appear on a 1099-K. Keep personal payments marked as personal (not "goods and services") in the apps and they stay out of the totals.

If you owe, don't wait for April

Nobody withholds tax from gig and selling income, so the IRS expects you to pay as you go once you'll owe about $1,000 or more for the year. The 2026 quarterly deadlines are April 15, June 15, September 15, and January 15 (2027). A simple rule that avoids penalties for most people: pay in at least as much as your entire tax was last year (110% of it if you earned over $150,000), spread over the four dates.
